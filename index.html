02 / Approach

How I think about research

My psychology studies have made me curious about behaviour. UX research gives that curiosity a practical question: what should a product team do differently?

01 / FRAME THE QUESTION

Understand the problem first

I start by asking what someone is trying to do and what the team actually needs to learn. A feature idea is not the same as a user need.

02 / CHOOSE THE EVIDENCE

Use the right method

Interviews can explain experiences. Surveys can explore patterns. Experiments can test a specific prediction. The question should guide the method.

03 / SUPPORT DECISIONS

Be clear about what is known

I try to separate observations, assumptions and recommendations. If something has not been tested, it should be described as a hypothesis.

03 / About

A little about me

Hi, I'm Leyla. I study Psychology at the University of Westminster, and I've always been curious about why people respond differently to the same experience. Why does one person find an app motivating while someone else finds it stressful? Those are the kinds of questions I enjoy exploring.

That's what drew me to UX research. I want to understand people's experiences before jumping to solutions. My degree is helping me build skills in research design, qualitative and quantitative methods and data analysis. I'm still learning, and I want this portfolio to be open about what I've done and what I'd like to explore next.

As a Course Representative, I listen to students' concerns and bring their feedback to staff. It's taught me how important it is to be approachable, especially when someone is frustrated or unsure how to explain what's wrong. I'm also President of the Women in STEM Society and an Adobe Student Ambassador, where I get to organise events and connect with students from different backgrounds.

I'd love to grow as a UX researcher, learn from experienced teams and help make digital experiences more considerate of the people using them.
